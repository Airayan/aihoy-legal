The essentials

You set the average speed you want to keep over a distance, and Averagers keeps showing what your average will be at the end if you carry on like this. Green: you're under your target. Red: carry on like this and you'd go over.

If you're held up on the way – a traffic jam, roadworks, a slow lorry – the app works out the highest pace you can keep for the rest of the distance without your average turning red. That's the "Max. speed for the rest" number. The posted speed limit always comes first: if the calculated value would be higher than allowed, the app shows that separately.

And if you're going faster than you need to, it tells you early, so you can ease off in time instead of finding out at the end.

When it helps

  • On longer trips, on motorways or main roads, when you want to keep a steady pace and know what average you'll arrive with.
  • On a route you drive often, such as your commute: save it once, and next time measuring starts by itself.
  • On a motorbike with a Bluetooth helmet: the phone announces your projected average, so you don't need to look.
  • When you want to drive more calmly and evenly: the app also tells you when, after a long stop, it's no longer worth trying to make up the time.

Getting started

  1. Target average: use − / + or the quick buttons (50, 70, 80, 90, 110, 130 km/h).
  2. Distance: the quick buttons add up – for example +50, +2, +1 = 53 km. Pressed one twice by mistake? ⌫ undoes the last one.
  3. START: the timer starts when you actually start moving, not when you press the button. It's fine to start while standing still.

Where several drivers have measured before, the start screen shows a community suggestion: start with the most common setting in one tap.

The measuring screen

Projected average
The big number: your average at the end of the distance if you keep your current pace.
Max. speed for the rest
The highest average pace for the remaining distance that keeps you under target. If it's above usual speed limits, "no limit" and a sign are shown – the road signs are what count then.
Current
Your speed right now, according to GPS.
Average so far
Your average from the start until now.
Remaining / time
How much of the distance is left, and how much time has passed.

On the go

  • Feel free to switch to navigation or music, or turn the screen off – measuring keeps running, shown by a permanent notification.
  • Floating windowPRO: when you switch to another app, a small card shows your projected average on top. You can drag it, and tapping it brings you back to Averagers. Turn it on in the settings.
  • Change on the go: tap the header of the measuring screen (the target and distance line) to change the target or the distance – for example if the stretch turns out to be 53 km, not 50.
  • "I'm at the finish": in the same place you can end the measurement right where you are. The distance becomes the distance you actually covered, and time spent standing at the end doesn't count – so you can save it as a stretch with the exact length.
  • Sound and vibration alerts each have their own button on the measuring screen.

Motorbike mode & voice announcements

In the settings, under "Motorbike mode and voice announcements":

  • Motorbike mode: bigger buttons you can press with gloves; vibration is off by default.
  • Voice announcementsPRO: they play through a Bluetooth helmet or the car speakers and lower your music meanwhile. You'll hear:
    • your projected average at regular intervals (every 1, 2 or 5 km, or every 1, 2 or 5 minutes),
    • when you turn red, and when you're back under target,
    • 5–10 km before the end, if your current pace would take you over target – with a suggestion to slow down or take a short break,
    • once, if after a long stop the target could only be reached by going much faster: don't chase it.
  • Use the Test button to hear it at home.

My stretches PRO

If you often drive the same road, save it once and measuring will start by itself from then on.

Save after a measurement

At the end of a measurement, tap "Save stretch" on the result screen and give it a name.

Mark it on the map

  1. "My stretches" → "New stretch on the map".
  2. Tap the start, on the road.
  3. Tap a point a little after it, on the road, in the direction you'll travel – this tells the app your direction.
  4. Tap the end. Measuring will stop there.
  5. Enter a name, a target and the length along the road (from road signs or a route planner). After your first drive through, the app corrects it to the measured length by itself.

Stretch watch

Before you set off, start "Stretch watch" on the "My stretches" screen, then go ahead with navigation or music. When you pass the start in the saved direction, measuring starts by itself and stops at the end. No internet needed on the way, just GPS.

Tips

  • Use a phone holder, and set the app up before you drive.
  • Allow location and notifications, and for the floating window the "Display over other apps" permission – the app asks for these where they're needed.
  • In tunnels or with poor reception GPS may be inaccurate; measuring carries on and catches up on the distance once reception is back.
  • On longer trips, keep the phone on a charger – continuous GPS and an active screen use battery.
  • Pro features are free to try for now.

Important: Averagers is a tool for calculating average speed – it is not meant to help anyone break traffic rules. You must fully obey the traffic rules and drive according to road conditions while using it; road signs always take priority. GPS-based data is for information only.
